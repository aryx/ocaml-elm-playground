Games

Shoot 'em up

games/shmup/: a ship, or a gun, against waves of enemies, the screen scrolling past or the enemies coming down at you; you shoot and dodge, and a level is a timeline of waves. Not a deathmatch against other fighters in one arena (arcade and party).

TinyInvaders 2D, 1978
A formation of aliens marching down, and a cannon under four bunkers. source
Asteroid 2D, 1979
A ship with inertia, rocks breaking into smaller rocks. source
TinyMissileCommand 2D, 1980
Missiles rain on six cities; aim counter-missiles where they will be. source
TinyGalaga 2D, 1981
A fixed shooter whose enemies fly in on paths and dive at you. source
TinyDefender 2D, 1981
A planet six screens wide, humanoids to save from the landers. source
TinyRobotron 2D, 1982
Two sticks, one to run and one to shoot, and a crowd of robots. source
TinyZaxxon 2.5D, 1982
A fighter along an isometric fortress, over walls and between towers. source
TinyGradius 2D, 1985
A cave scrolling by itself, waves of enemies, and the power-up bar. source
TinyRType 2D, 1987
The Force at your ship's nose, a beam to charge, a battleship at the end. source
TinyStarFox 3D, 1993
An arwing down a canyon on rails: dodge and shoot. source

Beat 'em up and fighting

games/fighting/: the fighters' own mechanics are the game -- moves timed in frames, hitboxes against hurtboxes, blocks, combos, a body as a skeleton of joints; one against one, or one against waves of thugs down a street.

TinyFinalFight 2D, 1989
Walk down a street beating up wave after wave of thugs, then their boss. source
TinyStreetFighter 2D, 1991
Two fighters, one screen, best of three rounds, and a fireball. source
TinyVirtuaFighter 3D, 1993
Two fighters of flat-shaded boxes on a ring you can be knocked out of. source

Arcade and party games

games/arcade/: one screen, or one arena (or a screen wrapping round), with nothing to explore and no level to cross; one rule learned in seconds; short rounds, played again at once, for a score, the last one standing or the first to five; nothing carried from one round to the next; often several players in the same arena.

Two ships duelling around a star whose gravity pulls them in. source
Pong 2D, 1972

TinyLunarLander 2D, 1979

Card games

games/cards/: a deck of cards and the rules of a game played with it -- patience alone, or a table of players; what shows and what is hidden, and the luck of the deal, are the game. Every deal has a number (Microsoft's, the cards kit's), so a game can be played again.

TinyFreeCell 2D, 1978
All 52 cards face up on eight columns, and four free cells to park them. source
TinySolitaire 2D, 1990
Seven columns, the stock, and four foundations to build, ace to king. source

Sports and tables

games/sports/: a sport or a table game played on the screen as it is played off it -- a ball or a puck, a pitch or a table, its physics and its rules (or, like Speedball's, rules it could have), and a team or an opponent to beat. The table games count: pinball and air hockey (Shufflepuck) are simulated tables. Pong is not here: it only borrowed tennis's idea, two paddles and a ball, and is an arcade game.

TinyTennisForTwo 2D, 1958
Tennis from the side on an oscilloscope, a knob and a button each. source
TinyShufflePuck 2.5D, 1988
Air hockey down a table in perspective against a bar's regulars. source
TinyKickOff2 2D, 1990
Football from above, where the ball is not glued to your feet. source
TinySpeedball2 2D, 1990

TinySensibleSoccer 2D, 1992

TinyPinball 2D, 1992

TinyPinball3d 3D, 1992
The same table in 3D, on the physics engine. source
TinyTonyHawk 2D, 1999
Two minutes on a half-pipe seen from the side: pump, fly, flip, grab, and chain it all. source

Programming games

games/programming/: you win by writing the program that plays -- the player is a programmer, the game a machine and its rules, and a match is programs run against the machine or against each other.

TinyKarel 2D, 1981
Type a program in Pattis's language and watch the robot run it: the newspaper, the harvest, the stairs, the maze. source
TinyCoreWar 2D, 1984
Write two programs in its editor, then watch them fight in one circular memory, each trying to make the other execute a DAT. source
