The first editor where the screen looked like the page.
after Bravo (Butler Lampson and Charles Simonyi, Xerox PARC, 1974)
source
TinyWord (app, 1983)
The same text as TinyBravo, with no modes.
after Microsoft Word (1983 on DOS, 1985 on the Macintosh)
source
TinyFrameMaker (app, 1986)
A long document that lays itself out in columns and pages.
after FrameMaker (Charles Corfield, Frame Technology, 1986)
source

Graphics

apps/graphics/: the programs pictures are made with -- the two ways of keeping one, as dots (TinyMacPaint) and as objects (TinyMacDraw), both Apple's of 1984; the Amiga's dots, each a colour's number in a palette that can turn (TinyDeluxePaint); and what the dots became with 24 bits each (TinyPhotoshop, over libs/graphics/imaging: plan_photoshop.md); and a 3D scene built and ray traced (TinyBlender, over appkits/modeler and the Povray way).

TinyMacPaint (app, 1984)
Paint with dots: pencil, brush, shapes, bucket, and a selection to move.
after MacPaint (Bill Atkinson, Apple, 1984)
source
TinyMacDraw (app, 1984)
A picture made of objects you can select, move and group.
after MacDraw (Apple, 1984)
source
TinyDeluxePaint (app, 1985)
A picture of 32 colours whose waterfall and fire move by the palette alone: Tab, and the colours cycle.
after Deluxe Paint (Dan Silva, Electronic Arts, 1985, on the Amiga)
source
TinyPhotoshop (app, 1990)
NASA's Blue Marble to retouch: select with the magic wand, adjust Levels or Hue/Saturation, filter, paint.
after Photoshop 1.0 (Thomas and John Knoll, Adobe, 1990)
source
TinyBlender (app, 1994)
Blender's default cube in the quad view, moved with G, R and S, the camera's view ray traced live.
after Blender 2.8 (Ton Roosendaal, NeoGeo 1994, open source 2002; 2.8 in 2019)
source

CAD

apps/cad/: the drawings that are models rather than pictures -- geometry that keeps its own rules, constraints the program maintains, parts placed as instances of other drawings (plan_cad.md), faces that appear where lines close a loop.

TinySketchpad (app, 1963)
Draw with the light pen, then tell the drawing its rules: a rough hexagon becomes regular, a linkage moves.
after Sketchpad (Ivan Sutherland, MIT Lincoln Laboratory's TX-2, 1963)
source
TinyAutoCAD (app, 1992)
A bracket drawn on layers, by a command line that asks and a crosshair that snaps.
after AutoCAD Release 12 (Autodesk, 1992; AutoCAD 1982)
source
TinySketchup (app, 2000)
A house pushed and pulled out of a rectangle: draw a line across a face, pull the face, lift the ridge.
after SketchUp (Brad Schell and Joe Esch, @Last Software, 2000; Google SketchUp 8, 2010)
source

Education

apps/education/: the programs made for a learner of a subject -- the sky, geometry, logic, typing -- rather than for getting work done. Learning to program is elsewhere (the programming games, the ways, the programming tools), and so are the educational games, which are played.

TinyInteractivePhysics (app, 1989)
Draw disks and blocks, tie them with springs, ropes, rods and pins, press Run: the meters plot the energies, the tape rewinds.
after Interactive Physics (Knowledge Revolution: David Baszucki and Erik Cassel, 1989) and Working Model (1993)
source
TinyStellarium (app, 2001)
The sky of a place at an instant, as you would see it standing there; time runs as fast as you like, the sky turns, the Moon waxes and the planets wander.
after Stellarium (Fabien Chereau, 2001), after the planetarium projector (Zeiss Mark I, 1923)
source

PIM

TinyCalendar (app, 1971)
A month or a week of events, dragged, stretched and repeated; September 1752 as England lived it.
after cal (Unix, 1971), iCal (Apple, 2002)
source
TinyClock (app, 1984)
The time here and in six cities, on hands or in a strip, and an alarm.
after the Alarm Clock (Apple, Macintosh, 1984), xclock (X Window System, mid-1980s)
source
TinyPalmPilot (app, 1996)
A Date Book, an Address book, a To Do list and a Memo Pad behind four buttons, on a 160 x 160 screen.
after the Pilot (Jeff Hawkins, Donna Dubinsky and Ed Colligan, Palm Computing, 1996)
source

after Aseprite (David Capello, 2001)
source
TinyTiled (app, 2008)

Music

apps/music/: instruments played live, their voices built from audio/'s blocks (plan_synth_teaching.md), and the tracker.

TinyHammond (app, 1955)
Nine drawbars pulled into a registration, chords on the computer's keys, the Leslie's horn and drum turning slow or fast.
after Hammond B-3 (1955) and its Leslie 122 (1965)
source
TinyRhodes (app, 1970)
The electric pianos: a Rhodes to play softly or hard, its pickup's curve drawn with the tine's swing across it, the Suitcase's tremolo moving between its speakers.
after Fender Rhodes Mark I (1970) and its Suitcase; the Wurlitzer 200A (1974) and the Clavinet D6 (1971)
source
TinyMinimoog (app, 1970)
Three oscillators, the ladder filter and two contours, played from the computer's keys.
after Minimoog Model D (Moog Music, 1970)
source
TinyCS80 (app, 1977)
Vangelis's synthesizer: two synthesizers per key, a chord held and one of its notes pressed harder to open it alone, a ribbon to bend them.
after Yamaha CS-80 (1977)
source
TinyTR808 (app, 1980)
The drum machine of electro, hip hop and house: eleven drums with their knobs, the 16 coloured step buttons, the whole pattern shown as a grid.
after Roland TR-808 Rhythm Composer (1980)
source
TinyTB303 (app, 1981)
A 16-step bass line playing itself, its pattern a grid to click, its knobs turned while it plays: acid.
after Roland TB-303 Bass Line (1981)
source
TinyDX7 (app, 1983)
The FM synthesizer of the 1980s: 145 parameters edited one at a time on its LCD, as the DX7 made you, beside what it hid -- the algorithm drawn as a graph lit as it plays, the six envelopes; its cartridges read.
after Yamaha DX7 (1983)
source
TinyJuno (app, 1984)
The polyphonic synthesizer everyone could afford: its sliders in their sections, six voices, and the chorus that makes one oscillator sound like an orchestra.
after Roland Juno-106 (1984)
source
TinySoundtracker (app, 1987)
A song as a grid of notes in four channels, typed on a piano of letters, played as it's edited.
after Ultimate Soundtracker (Karsten Obarski, Amiga, 1987)
source
TinyReBirth (app, 1997)
The techno studio in a computer: two TB-303s, a TR-808 and a TR-909 playing together, each machine's pattern chosen, mixed, through distortion, delay, compressor and the pattern controlled filter.
after ReBirth RB-338 (Propellerhead, 1997)
source
TinyOp1 (app, 2011)
The synthesizer and four-track studio the size of a keyboard: six engines, an envelope, an effect and an LFO, each on the same four coloured encoders, eight sounds, and a tape recording what you play over what it plays back.
after OP-1 (Teenage Engineering, 2011)
source
TinyReface (app, 2015)
Four small keyboards in one case: a switch for the combo organ, the electric piano, FM and virtual analog, each our original behind it -- the Hammond, the Rhodes, the DX7, the CS-80 -- on 37 mini keys.
after Reface YC, CP, DX and CS (Yamaha, 2015)
source
TinyOpxy (app, 2024)
The groovebox: eight tracks stepping together -- an 808 kit sampled, a bass, sampled electric piano chords, a lead -- their 16 steps, parameter locks, scenes switched at the bar, and the brain moving the song into another key and scale.
after OP-XY (Teenage Engineering, 2024)
source

Media

apps/media/: programs for files of every kind -- sounds, tunes, pictures, and videos as graphics/videos/ comes (plan_video_teaching.md).

TinyMediaPlayer (app, 1991)
One player for every file this repository reads: recordings (WAV, MP2, MP3), tunes, modules, pictures, an animation, a video, and an .mpg with its sound.
after Media Player (Microsoft, 1991), VLC (VideoLAN, 2001)
source
TinyWinamp (app, 1998)
The MP3 player of the MP3 years in its three windows: the LCD time, the title scrolling, the analyzer, the ten-band equalizer and the playlist.
after Winamp 2 (Nullsoft, 1998)
source

Internet

apps/internet/: programs that talk to other computers, over networking/, the servers they talk to small enough to run beside them.

TinyEudora (app, 1988)
Mailboxes and a message: the list with Eudora's columns, a message read under it, its headers all shown with b, its attachment saved; a reply written, queued in Out, and sent; Check Mail; your own Gmail; the list by conversation, filters.
after Eudora (Steve Dorner, University of Illinois, 1988)
source
TinyIRC (app, 1989)
Channels and nicks: join #tiny, type a line, everyone in it reads it.
after ircII (Michael Sandrof, 1989), for IRC (Jarkko Oikarinen, 1988)
source
TinyMosaic (app, 1993)
A web browser: a page fetched, read and drawn, its links followed.
after NCSA Mosaic (Marc Andreessen and Eric Bina, 1993)
source
TinyNetscape (app, 1994)
Mosaic's successor: a toolbar, a Location field to type in, pictures arriving four at a time.
after Netscape Navigator 1.0 (Marc Andreessen and the Mosaic Communications team, 1994)
source
TinyFirefox (app, 2004)
A browser that runs the page's own program: click, type, and watch the console and the page's tree change.
after Firefox 1.0 (the Mozilla Foundation, 2004), and Firebug (Joe Hewitt, 2006)
source
TinyChrome (app, 2008)
A small browser for the real web: Hacker News, Wikipedia (its search from the omnibox), Google's home page, GitHub, drawn mostly as their authors meant, in tabs, with developer tools; Hacker News' own script run, a thread folded; TinyTube, a video site of our own.
after Google Chrome (2008)
source

System

apps/system/: the programs a computer is used through before any application -- so far the terminal and its shell, over the playground's Teletype way and libs/terminal/ (plan_terminal.md).

TinyTerminal (app, 1978)
A screen of 80 by 24 and a prompt: type a command, run Hangman, stop it with Control-C; basic, then RUN MANDEL.
after the VT100 (DEC, 1978), the Unix shell (Ken Thompson, 1971; Stephen Bourne, 1979)
source

Programming tools

apps/devtools/: the programs programs are written with -- so far a BASIC as a home computer had it, over languages/basic (plan_terminal.md, section 5), and the two text editors, full-screen programs of the terminal over appkits/editor (section 6), Emacs's Lisp over languages/lisp, Turbo Pascal's IDE over languages/pascal, Smalltalk-80's environment over languages/smalltalk (plan_tiny_smalltalk.md), and Scratch's blocks and Snap!'s over languages/scratch and appkits/blocks.

TinyBasic (app, 1975)
Type a program a numbered line at a time, LIST it, RUN it; FP for floating point; Guess the Number is typed in already.
after Tiny BASIC (Dennis Allison, 1975; Li-Chen Wang, Palo Alto, 1976) and Applesoft (Microsoft, 1978), on the Apple II's screen (Steve Wozniak's Integer BASIC, 1977)
source
TinyVi (app, 1976)
A screen editor with modes: keys are commands until i, text until Escape; README says them.
after vi (Bill Joy, Berkeley, 1976), on ex and ed (Ken Thompson, 1969)
source
TinySmalltalk80 (app, 1980)
A System Browser, a Workspace and a Transcript on a grey screen: print it on 100 factorial, accept a method into Point, and when a message is not understood, define it in the debugger and proceed.
after Smalltalk-80 (Alan Kay, Dan Ingalls, Adele Goldberg, Xerox PARC, 1980; Byte, August 1981; the Blue Book, 1983)
source
TinyTurboPascal (app, 1983)
Wirth's eight queens in a blue window: F9 compiles, Ctrl-F9 runs, the error in a red bar with the cursor on it.
after Turbo Pascal (Anders Hejlsberg, Borland, 1983), in the look of Turbo Pascal 7 (1992)
source
TinyEmacs (app, 1985)
An editor that is a Lisp machine: type (+ 1 2) then C-j in *scratch*, or C-h k and a key to see what it runs.
after GNU Emacs (Richard Stallman, 1985), after EMACS on TECO (1976)
source
TinyScratch (app, 2007)
Click the green flag: the cat walks and bounces while the pencil draws a flower; drag blocks from the palette to change them, click a script to run it.
after Scratch (Mitchel Resnick, John Maloney, Natalie Rusk and the Lifelong Kindergarten group, MIT Media Lab, 2007), in the look of Scratch 2.0 (2013)
source
TinySnap (app, 2011)
Click the green flag: the turtle draws a tree by a recursive block of its own, then says the squares of one to six, by map; click it for 10 factorial.
after Snap! (Jens Mönig and Brian Harvey, UC Berkeley, 2011; BYOB, 2008), in the look of Snap! 4 (2015)
source
